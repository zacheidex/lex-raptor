Legal research, on your terms.

Lex Raptor brings cases, regulations, and source-linked analysis into a conversation.

Ask a question, attach a document, brief a case, compare authorities, or build a timeline. The model chooses a research task and search scope from your message. You can set those choices yourself at any time.

Built to stay open

Lex Raptor is licensed under AGPL-3.0. You can inspect the source, run it yourself, and build on it under the license. Your access to the open-source software does not depend on a paid account.

Explore the repository or download the source.

Local or connected

Run the model on your computer with Ollama for no model API fees. The website uses a hosted model, with a shared $10 total AI allowance while we test. No account is required. Optional managed API services are planned; customer billing is not currently enabled.

Authority you can inspect

Every retained finding links to a quotation from retrieved source text. You can read the passage, open the original record, and export your research. Quote matching checks the words; it does not establish that a conclusion follows from them.

Lex Raptor does not provide KeyCite or Shepard’s treatment analysis. Review legal reasoning, jurisdiction, and current validity before relying on an answer. See sources and coverage.

Transparent evaluation

We publish our development prompts, results, and observed failures. The current evaluation is not a commercial-product comparison or an independently measured legal-accuracy score.

Read the research evaluation.

Your research

Chat history and attached documents stay in the current browser tab’s memory and is cleared when you reload or start a new chat. On the website, messages, recent conversation context, and selected extracted document passages go to OpenAI when you send a request. Files are parsed on your device; original files are not uploaded. Search terms go to the selected databases. The app caches public source records and stores usage accounting, but does not save conversations. Providers may retain requests under their own policies. Use public or hypothetical facts.
