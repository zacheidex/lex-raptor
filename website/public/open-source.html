What makes us different

The datasets behind the research

Lex Raptor connects to public legal sources and includes a small offline case library. The code is open source; each provider’s data, access terms and coverage remain its own. Results show which sources were actually used.

CourtListener Cases · online

Federal and state opinions, full opinion text and citation resolution through Free Law Project’s API. Coverage varies by court and date. Local installations need their own API token; service quotas and membership terms apply.

Exact citations are resolved before briefing. Topic research can use semantic search; named cases and custom queries use keyword search. Case-status requests perform a limited search for later opinions, without comprehensive treatment analysis.

CourtListener data and API access
eCFR Federal regulations · online

Federal regulations with dated source text. The eCFR is an editorial compilation of CFR material and amendments, distinct from the official legal edition. It does not supply state statutes or the U.S. Code.

About the eCFR publication
Federal Register Agency publications · online

Published rules, proposed rules, notices and presidential documents, with source records and original PDF links when available. A proposal or notice is not automatically binding law.

Explore the Federal Register
Public legal web Statutes and codes · hosted AI

Searches selected government sites and legal publishers for statutes, municipal codes and public guidance. Findings retain source links and available provision identifiers, such as a code section, with editions when known.

This route uses the hosted model’s paid web tool within the shared budget. It is not used by local Ollama or source-only search. Coverage is selective; an unofficial mirror or older edition stays labeled. Check citations reopens pages and reviews their support, reporting unreadable or unsupported material.

CAP starter library 12 Supreme Court cases · offline

A bundled selection of twelve U.S. Supreme Court opinions from Harvard’s Caselaw Access Project, available for local research. This is a starter library, not the full CAP archive or a current nationwide database.

About the Caselaw Access Project
Your documents PDF, DOCX, text and Markdown

Attach a document for source-linked analysis or to collect its cited cases. Text is extracted in your browser; scanned PDFs need OCR first. Document-only analysis is the default. Include outside research under Refine → Attached documents.

In the hosted demo, submitted text goes to the hosted server and OpenAI for analysis. Local inference uses your local model; enabled online databases still receive queries. Original attachment files are not stored. Explicitly saved browser-local projects can contain excerpts, questions, answers and notes, and do not sync across devices.

Google Scholar Case law · external search

Choose Google Scholar in the Auto task menu to open a case-law search, or follow a case’s Scholar link in the evidence panel. Set courts and dates in Scholar. Copy a reporter citation back into Find a case or Collect cited cases to resolve it through CourtListener.

Scholar’s official help says bulk access is unavailable. This integration opens Scholar for you; results are not automatically retrieved, imported or treated as verified evidence.

Give your raptor a home.

Run the chat and a local Ollama model on your computer. The web workbench needs Node.js 20 or later and an installed model; the setup check reports missing configuration. Hardware needs depend on the model you choose.

Use the offline library and your documents without an online model service. Add database credentials when you want live research. Local inference does not make online searches offline: those providers still receive your queries.
