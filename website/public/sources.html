Know your sources.

The research chat searches all connected online databases by default. Choose a narrower scope, or enable automatic database selection.

CourtListener

Search federal and state case law, retrieve opinion text, and look up citations through Free Law Project’s authenticated API. The website’s connection is active. Local installations use your own API token.

Coverage varies by court and date. Automatic topic searches use CourtListener’s semantic search to find opinions about the question; named-case searches and manual queries use keyword search. Explicit single-state law questions can be scoped automatically to that state’s appellate courts; federal interpretations and trial decisions may be missed. Manual court and query selections take precedence. Named-case searches prioritize the principal decision and lead opinion. Case-status questions also search for later treatment, including recent opinions, reading up to four full opinion records. Relevant or adverse cases may still be missed; this is not a comprehensive citator. Citation lookup checks existence and ambiguity, not later treatment.

eCFR

Search federal regulations and retrieve dated section text without a data subscription. Results identify the snapshot date. eCFR is an editorial compilation, not the official legal edition.

Explore eCFR

Federal Register

Search published rules, proposed rules, notices, and presidential documents. Results include source type and official PDF links where available. A proposal is not a final rule.

Explore the Federal Register

Public legal web

The hosted chat can search public statutes, government guidance and municipal codes. Findings link to government websites or selected legal publishers: Cornell LII, Justia statutes and opinions, CourtListener opinions, Municode, American Legal Publishing and eCode360. Check each source’s edition, effective date and any later amendments.

These are provider web citations, clearly labeled in the answer. Their page text is not independently downloaded or quote-checked by Lex Raptor. Web research is selective, not a complete statutory database. It uses up to four paid search-tool calls within the shared $10 AI allowance. Local Ollama and the Source search button never invoke this paid tool.

About the original collection

Your documents

Attach PDFs, DOCX files, plain text or Markdown. Document-only mode uses their extracted text without searching a public database. You can explicitly include database research in Search settings. Uploaded text is user-provided material, not independently verified authority.

Questions and coverage gaps

When a missing detail would materially change the answer, chat asks a focused follow-up before searching. You can reply in your own words or request a general overview. Clear questions proceed directly to research.

Public legal web broadens access to state statutes, city ordinances and the U.S. Code. It does not guarantee complete or current coverage. Court opinions may discuss older statutes; eCFR and the Federal Register cover federal regulations and agency publications. For a rule you plan to rely on, check the current official code and effective dates, or attach its text for analysis. Comprehensive foreign law coverage is not available.

Scope and verification

Search results disclose the databases searched, retrieved passages, dates, and partial failures. Live searches read up to three full records per database, or four case opinions for status questions, and use selected excerpts for drafting. For downloaded passages, the model selects an exact text segment and the server checks its source. For web citations, the server verifies the URL appeared in the search service’s results and belongs to an eligible source. Invalid references are removed. Neither check establishes legal support or correct interpretation. Broader searches and source-by-source review remain necessary.

Student access

Free Law Project offers complimentary memberships for eligible students with an .edu address. Apply through its membership page for expanded API access. Your law library can also confirm school access to Westlaw/CoCounsel and Lexis+ AI for your own research and comparisons.

Public eCFR and Federal Register searches require no paid subscription. Provider quotas still apply. Lex Raptor does not purchase PACER records or upgrade your data plan automatically.
